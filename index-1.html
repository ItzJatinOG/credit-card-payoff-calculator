How the calculator works

Each month your card charges interest equal to your APR divided by 12, multiplied by the current balance. Your payment covers that interest first, and whatever is left reduces what you owe. As the balance shrinks, less interest is charged, so more of each payment goes to principal.

This tool repeats that calculation month by month until the balance reaches zero. It assumes a fixed rate, a fixed payment, and no new purchases or fees.

Example

A 5,000 balance at 22% APR with a 200 monthly payment takes about 34 months and costs roughly 1,700 in interest. Raising the payment by 20% cuts about seven months and roughly 350 in interest. Try it above with your own numbers.

Ways to pay off a card faster

  • Pay more than the minimum, even by a small amount. The extra goes straight to principal.
  • Put windfalls such as bonuses or tax refunds toward the balance.
  • Ask your issuer for a lower APR, or move the balance to a lower-rate option if the fees make sense.
  • Stop adding new purchases to the card while you pay it down.

Frequently asked questions

How is credit card interest calculated?

Most cards apply the APR divided by 12 to your balance each month. Some issuers compound daily, so your real cost can be slightly higher than shown.

Why does paying only the minimum take so long?

Minimum payments are often only slightly above the monthly interest, so the balance barely falls and interest keeps building.

How much extra should I pay each month?

Whatever fits your budget. Use the tip box in the results to see what a 20% higher payment would save.

Does this include fees or new purchases?

No. Annual fees, late fees and new spending are not included, so treat results as an estimate.

About PayoffClock

PayoffClock is a free tool that shows how long it takes to clear a credit card and how much interest you will pay along the way. Enter your balance, interest rate and payment, and the result appears instantly.

It uses standard monthly compounding: your APR divided by 12 is applied to the balance each month, then your payment is deducted. Fees, new purchases and daily compounding are not included, so results are estimates.

PayoffClock provides general information only and is not financial advice. For decisions about your debt, speak to a qualified financial advisor.
